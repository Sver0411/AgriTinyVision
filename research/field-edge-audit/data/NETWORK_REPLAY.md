# Mahimahi batch-transfer service replay

Three pairs of mobile driving traces from the Mahimahi distribution give service opportunities for representative historical cellular links. AT&T LTE, T-Mobile LTE and Verizon EVDO each contribute one uplink file and one downlink file. Every line is one 1,500-byte opportunity at its integer millisecond timestamp. Repeated timestamps allow several packets through in a millisecond. The six files are exact copies from revision `f1346c38c415abfc98626dd82450f49a57033603`. The manifest records individual source URLs, byte counts, timestamps and SHA-256 digests. The repository includes GNU GPL version 3 in `COPYING` and declares `GPL-3+` for `Files: *` in `debian/copyright`. Both files are preserved with the traces. [Fixed revision and trace description](https://github.com/ravinet/mahimahi/tree/f1346c38c415abfc98626dd82450f49a57033603/traces) · [Upstream licensing declaration](https://github.com/ravinet/mahimahi/blob/f1346c38c415abfc98626dd82450f49a57033603/debian/copyright) · [Original Saturator study](https://www.usenix.org/conference/nsdi13/technical-sessions/presentation/winstein)

We replayed both strategies at 20 calibration seeds, label budgets 32 and 128, local labeling and remote image review, and deployment batches of 1, 10, 100 and 1,353 images. Both strategies use the same first H held-out paths in saved split order. Every image contributes the exact length of its 224-pixel JPEG plus its application header. The deployment simulation charges the local update one 1,293-byte calibration record per labeled vector and a single 1,294-byte returned head. With remote review the local method also sends calibration images with an eight-byte header per image. The remote DINO strategy uploads labeled calibration JPEGs with nine-byte headers and held-out JPEGs with eight-byte headers, then downloads one 12-byte returned score record per held-out image. At H = 1,353 all scenario totals match the original experiment’s per-seed byte ledger.

All application uploads are ready together at a fixed start time. A batch barrier makes all downlink responses available after the complete upload. For uploaded bytes, we consume opportunities at or after batch start. The downlink begins strictly after the millisecond in which the upload completes. Within a direction application records share packet capacity, and the last packet can be partial. Time spent training, deciding or reviewing, propagation delay, handshakes, headers below the application layer, congestion control, retransmission, and concurrent traffic are omitted. The reported numbers quantify idealized batch service on recorded links. Real-time interaction and agricultural sites require observations of their own.

Each direction retains its original time axis. The starts are fixed at 0, 1/8, 2/8, 3/8 and 4/8 of the paired directions' common duration, rounded down in milliseconds. Every transfer finished within both original recordings. Each summary group has 20 seeds × 5 phase pairs. These are repeated workloads on three recorded traces and are appropriate for descriptive comparisons. Row-level data contain service times in milliseconds; the summary contains seconds, four descriptive quantiles/endpoints, counts of wins and ties, and paired differences.

| 128 remotely reviewed labels | Local / DINO service, one image (median seconds) | Local / DINO service, 1,353 images (median seconds) |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| AT&T LTE driving | 8.294 / 6.879 | 8.294 / 87.656 |
| T-Mobile LTE driving | 0.515 / 0.406 | 0.515 / 3.138 |
| Verizon EVDO driving | 9.206 / 7.661 | 9.206 / 91.896 |

At H = 1 across both labeling arrangements and budgets, the local head had shorter service in 600 pairs, the results tied in 80 and DINO had shorter service in 520. At H = 1,353, the local head had shorter service in all 1,200 pairs. Detailed results and full input identity are in `results/mahimahi_runs.csv`, `results/mahimahi_summary.csv` and `results/mahimahi_manifest.json`.

Run `python scripts/mahimahi_batch.py` from the project root with NumPy installed. The built-in synthetic check uses equally spaced 1,500-byte service opportunities 10 ms apart, beginning at 10 ms. A 3,001-byte upload uses opportunities at 10, 20 and 30 ms. A subsequent 1,501-byte download uses opportunities at 40 and 50 ms, giving exactly 50 ms of service from a start time of zero.
