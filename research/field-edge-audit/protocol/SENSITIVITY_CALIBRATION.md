# Locked high-sensitivity operating point

This ablation reuses the frozen Cornell split and the original 20 nested 128-label draws. For seed s, the first 64 labels fit a classifier and the last 64 calibrate a threshold. No held-out labels enter either fit. The unchanged frozen binary model also receives a threshold-only comparator which uses the last 64 labels and no training labels. This comparator therefore consumes only 64 labels. Each learned model has two rows, one with its native zero threshold and one after calibration. The frozen model's production threshold is 0.30 on suspect probability. Its threshold-only comparator uses its raw suspect-minus-normal logit. AUROC for each is evaluated on the signed unrounded raw margin to avoid ties caused solely by a float32 probability saturating to one.

Calibration scores come from the serialized, reread trained head. Among m calibration positives, sort scores in descending order and set the threshold to the score of the ceil(19m/20)-th positive. Inclusive comparison at that value includes tied scores. This is the highest observed cutoff retaining at least 95% empirical sensitivity. Healthy labels do not determine this cutoff. When no positive is available, set the cutoff to negative infinity, issue an alert on every image, and flag the case. A fixed and changing threshold on the same score has identical AUROC. For these comparisons AUROC receives the real-valued score before thresholding and awards a half credit for a tie. For the original small-budget table its frozen model uses the recorded raw suspect-minus-normal margin; the other models use the decoded signed score as recorded.

These are empirical sensitivity and specificity on one locked test pool. A threshold meeting an empirical target on a small positive calibration subset does not establish a population guarantee or a lower confidence bound of 95%. Outcomes from the 20 training/calibration samples share the same test set. The 64 calibration labels are distinct from the first 64 labels and form random draws without balancing within the 128 sampled candidate images. Images represent field-labeled disease status. Scores and missed alerts do not directly measure disease severity or agronomic losses.

Run `python scripts/sensitivity_calibration.py` after generating the original target-adaptation trace. The `results/sensitivity64_metrics.csv` and `results/sensitivity64_summary.csv` files report per-seed and aggregate operating points. The `results/sensitivity64_predictions.npz` file includes both halves of each draw, the resulting thresholds and scores, and the complete held-out decisions. Its scores use raw margins or signed logits for every model. The `results/sensitivity64_heads.zip` archive stores the 60 heads trained on the first 64 labels. The two files with prefix `results/edge_adaptation_auroc` provide the earlier main experiment's test AUROCs. The independent `test_sensitivity_calibration.py` checks empirical maximality of every chosen cutoff, split disjointness, every binary decision and rank-sum AUROC.

For the completed split the positive calibration sample contained 41–51 images. Eleven of the 20 calibrated INT8 heads met 0.95 sensitivity on the fixed test set. The corresponding counts were nine for the original margin, nine for scalar calibration, and two for the calibrated remote DINO. The original frozen threshold itself achieved 0.965 test sensitivity and 0.430 specificity on this particular population. INT8 calibration increased mean test sensitivity from 0.861 to 0.942 and reduced mean specificity from 0.699 to 0.533. The fitting stage improved mean AUROC from the frozen 0.768 to 0.900, and adjusting the threshold preserved that ranking. Every threshold-only raw-margin decision equaled the corresponding calibrated 64-label scalar decision on this holdout. The native and adjusted operating points of the DINO model had identical mean AUROC of 0.904, while mean sensitivity increased from 0.836 to 0.921.
