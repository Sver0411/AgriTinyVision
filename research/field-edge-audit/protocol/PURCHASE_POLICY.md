# A camera's paid-label decision

This is a follow-up to earlier results on these image collections, registered as a fixed local specification before running the present policy on held-out outcomes. The known site failures motivated the design. This chronology provides no independent external validation.

The decision function receives a small, distinct paid target-site audit panel, the candidate predictions on that panel, a hard remaining-byte balance and each action's incremental price. It does not receive a site name, backbone name, test labels, test inputs, test prediction vectors, class prevalence or future uploader outcomes. The benchmark fixes the same label ceiling (32 or 128) and initial grant within a matched cell. Charge all pilot JPEGs, metadata and measured activation/head transactions to the same balance as the subsequent review requests. The 16 paid pilot images are disjoint from evaluation. Small and large pilot heads use nested fitting rows within each outer holdout fold; the held-out pilot image contributes to neither head. Remote predictions on a purchased calibration JPEG are legitimate free computational byproducts of that same charged transfer. When quote or support is insufficient the affordable baseline continues. A skipped or stopped purchase retains its bytes for subsequent causal review.

## Decision rule

Compare keeping the source head, installing the pilot head and buying the remaining allowed labels to install the larger head. For each action we evaluate its complete, bandwidth-priced deployment behavior on the pilot panel. The expansion candidate's out-of-fold predictions use the pilot head as its present-day proxy with the smaller future review allowance. No additional calibration response is seen until expansion is selected. A paired validation comparison conditions separately on true negatives and positives. In each class let (w, l, t) count favorable, unfavorable and unchanged outcomes relative to freezing. A Dirichlet(1/2, 1/2, 1/2) working model gives mean gain (w-l)/(n+1.5) and variance (((w+l+1)/(n+1.5)) - mean²)/(n+2.5). Balanced accuracy is the average of these two conditional rates. We use the sum of class variances divided by four for its variance. These moments are a decision heuristic, not confidence guarantees for future sites.

Within the same pilot, compare small and large out-of-fold heads on identical rows, with a 2:1 fit-size ratio. An assumed saturation law of a minus b divided by square-root(n) forecasts additional gains. The expansion credit equals max(0, paired learning gain minus one SD) times min(1, (1-sqrt(16/L))/(sqrt(2)-1)) at ceiling L. Credit cannot exceed one observed training-size increment and costs are debited separately. The proxy may overestimate marginal benefit when domain or score ordering changes after installation. Report the actual larger head on independent test rows. If no paired learning evidence is supplied the credit is zero.

Keep an action only when its price fits, its pilot sensitivity is at least that of the reference and its conservative score (paired BA gain minus one SD plus the stated credit) is positive. At least two pilot examples of each class are required. Maximize the score, then prefer the cheaper action, then the lexicographically first action for an exact tie. The baseline is the default. No factor, threshold or prior is site-specific. Policy names ending in `_guard` preserve a positive source-camera prediction through both head replacement and remote review. This has a deterministic sensitivity consequence relative to the raw frozen camera: it cannot add a missed positive on any image. It can retain false positives. Both unprotected and equivalently protected static controls must be evaluated. The selector's sensitivity check on the pilot alone has no deployment guarantee.

The paired empirical selector maximizes observed pilot BA with no shrinkage, learning projection or sensitivity eligibility rule; perfect ties prefer cheaper configurations. The additional `without_recall_guard` comparison removes only the pilot sensitivity condition from the protected selector while retaining its conservative score. Numerical tests include data where expansion wins, a regime where the no-recall alternative raises pilot BA by sacrificing sensitivity, zero improvement, scarce minority support and unaffordable expansion. Pilot purchases and candidate expansion prices should also be inspected separately by site, byte allowance and backbone.
