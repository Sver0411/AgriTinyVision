# Audit of previously fixed decisions

The endpoint requires both error counts to be strictly below the released AgriTinyVision FP32 classifier on the same set of images. The Cornell group-disjoint holdout has 1,353 photographs, 377 healthy and 976 diseased, with baseline FP=215 and FN=34. The fixed Kashmir cohort has 384 processed images, 128 healthy and 256 diseased, with baseline FP=81 and FN=4. The integer pass limits are therefore FP≤214 and FN≤33 on Cornell, FP≤80 and FN≤3 on Kashmir.

Run `python scripts/dual_existing.py` to recover `results/dual_existing.csv` and its grouped summary. No model is trained by this script. It pairs Cornell and Kashmir rows by the same fitted head, native or Cornell-calibrated threshold, and seed, and reuses all 20 original random label draws. The method registry contains the frozen local classifier, scalar, INT8 and FP32 local target heads, remote binary DINO target heads, the existing high-sensitivity calibration experiment, MobileNet at 224 pixels, the two original PlantDoc-trained binary specialists, and the original target-calibrated PlantDoc specialist margin. It contains 444 configurations, including several policies with identical decisions. These configurations and seeds are correlated. The number of successes is descriptive. No threshold or seed was selected by looking at Kashmir labels; the Kashmir pool had previously been inspected for descriptive transfer performance and evaluated processed images with unknown augmentation parents.

## Locked observations

The 64-fit plus 64-calibration INT8 policy passed in three of its 20 Cornell draws and two of its 20 Kashmir draws. Both Kashmir passing draws also passed Cornell. Draw 2 produced FP/FN counts of 199/33 on Cornell and 75/3 on Kashmir. Draw 4 produced 204/29 and 74/3. Draw 7 produced 197/33 and 72/4, which fails the strict Kashmir endpoint. The prespecified policy's mean counts were 176.15/56.70 on Cornell and 63.25/13.00 on Kashmir; both means have more missed cases than the corresponding original classifier. The passing seed identities were learned from this audit and cannot justify selecting those instances of the model for deployment.

None of the default 8/32/128-label scalar, INT8, FP32 or target-fitted DINO heads passed on Cornell. Among previously fitted remote comparators, one high-sensitivity DINO and three 8-label scalar calibrations of the original source-trained DINO passed on Cornell only. No source-only comparator passed both cohorts with its locked threshold. A one-case reduction of missed disease on the 384 processed Kashmir images is a finite-set observation, and broader sensitivity or safety does not follow.

## Diagnostic threshold envelope

For each existing score on each test cohort independently, sort descending and admit entire blocks of tied scores, including the initial all-negative decision. Under the strict baseline FP cap minimize missed positives over the resulting boundaries; if several boundaries achieve that value use the one with fewest FP. The `test_oracle_` columns report this exact finite-score possibility bound. The test labels enable the search, and the two cohorts can use different hypothetical boundaries. No oracle result describes a deployable threshold. The 128-label INT8 score admitted such a threshold on Cornell for 20/20 draws and on Kashmir for 18/20, while the originally supplied zero threshold passed neither cohort for any of these draws. The 64-label INT8 scores admitted such a boundary on Cornell for 20/20 and on Kashmir for 13/20; the independent 64-label Cornell calibration actually passed on both for 2/20.
